What the satellite actually saw

Flood monitoring is not limited by how fast the map arrives. It is limited by how rarely the satellite looks, relative to how fast the water moves. One method, four basins, two continents, a year of real acquisition metadata, and the same collection throughout. A free forecast already exists for every basin here. What is missing is an observation fast enough to check it.

07The forecast

A prediction already exists and it is free: GloFAS publishes a 30 day river discharge forecast for every basin on this page. So the useful question is not whether we can forecast. It is whether anything can check the forecast. Each row is a real forecast pulled today, next to the measured chance, averaged over the year, that a Sentinel-1 look lands within a day of a forecast peak. No future acquisition times exist, so this is a rate, not a prediction about a particular date.

08What this does not tell you

  1. No acquisition is not no flood. A gap means we did not look, not that water was absent. Nothing here should be read as zero.
  2. Product latency is not the constraint. Copernicus GFM publishes a global 20 m flood layer in under 8 hours for more than 99% of scenes. The limit is revisit, not processing.
  3. SAR has its own errors. CEMS reports over-detection from water look-alikes, and under-detection under dense vegetation, in wind, and in urban areas.
  4. Rise times are assumptions. 48 hours for a lowland river and 6 hours for a steep catchment are order-of-magnitude values, not per-event measurements. The sweep shows how the conclusion moves if they are wrong.
  5. The forecast covers rivers only. GloFAS states it gives no flash-flood, coastal, or inundation-extent forecast, so the case that fails here is also the case with no model to fall back on.
  6. Coverage is not equal, and the difference is large. Same collection, same window, same method: 315 looks a year over the Po, 250 over the Ahr, 78 over Riau, 51 over Jakarta. A basin's cadence sets the shortest flood it can see, which is why a week-long peat flood in Riau is caught nearly every time while a six-hour one in Jakarta almost never is.
  7. Europe is the optimistic case. Both European basins sit inside the Sentinel-1 European observation scenario, which plans them for frequent revisit. Four basins do not describe the world, but the Indonesian pair shows how far below the European figure coverage can fall.
